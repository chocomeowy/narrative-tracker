# Tax Intelligence Briefing - 2026-09-17 04:01

In late 2026, global corporate tax architectures and international trade frameworks are navigating deep structural transformations. The operationalization of the OECD Pillar Two 15% global minimum corporate tax has shifted from statutory adoption to active enforcement, demanding rigorous GloBE reporting from multinational enterprises. Concurrently, regional jurisdictions across ASEAN and Europe are actively adapting their tax regimes—exemplified by Singapore's YA 2026 Corporate Income Tax (CIT) rebates and cash grants—to maintain investment competitiveness under minimum tax thresholds. Digital transformation remains a critical policy priority, with tax administrations replacing periodic audits with Continuous Transaction Controls (CTC) via mandatory electronic invoicing networks, such as Singapore's Peppol-based GST InvoiceNow. Simultaneously, global supply chains are adapting to sustained tariff pressures, driven by unilateral trade modifications and active administrative adjustments to the Harmonized Tariff Schedule.
