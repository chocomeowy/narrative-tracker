# Tax Intelligence Briefing - 2026-09-21 16:02

The corporate and international tax policy landscape in mid-to-late 2026 is driven by the formalization of OECD Pillar Two, which has reached a phase of granular data reporting and compliance monitoring. With the release of the 2026 OECD Corporate Tax Statistics, tax administrations are now leveraging sophisticated data to audit MNE base erosion more effectively. Regional hubs, particularly in the ASEAN bloc, are navigating the dual pressures of global minimum tax compliance and FDI preservation by utilizing targeted rebates and incentive restructuring rather than simple rate cuts. Parallel to these fiscal shifts, the rapid digitization of tax collection via Peppol-standardized e-invoicing is becoming a mandatory operational baseline for enterprises. Conversely, the global trade landscape remains highly volatile as unilateral tariff strategies encounter significant, and occasionally successful, judicial challenges, creating a fractured outlook for international supply chain costs.
