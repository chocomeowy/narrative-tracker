# Tax Intelligence Briefing - 2026-09-21 10:01

The corporate and international tax policy landscape in mid-to-late 2026 is defined by the operational pressure of the OECD Pillar Two 15% global minimum tax, supported by newly released 2026 OECD Corporate Tax Statistics tracking worldwide rate shifts and BEPS mitigation. In response, key FDI hubs like Singapore are leveraging complex domestic corporate income tax rebates and structured incentives to maintain competitiveness alongside their 17% headline rate. Concurrently, digital compliance is accelerating with Singapore and other ASEAN jurisdictions scaling up mandatory Peppol-based e-invoicing (InvoiceNow) systems. On the trade front, legal challenges to unilateral trade policies have reached a critical juncture, underscored by the US Court of International Trade ruling against certain IEEPA-based tariffs, highlighting growing judicial resistance to unilateral tariff regimes.
