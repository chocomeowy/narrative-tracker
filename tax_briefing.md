# Tax Intelligence Briefing - 2026-09-10 10:01

The international tax and trade environment in late 2026 is defined by operational compliance under OECD Pillar Two and expanding digital tax reporting mandates, juxtaposed against persistent unilateral tariff escalations. Over 140 jurisdictions are actively executing Pillar Two global minimum tax (15%) frameworks, prompting sovereigns—particularly within ASEAN—to recalibrate corporate statutory rates and domestic incentive structures as detailed in the OECD's 2026 Corporate Tax Statistics. Concurrently, digital indirect tax enforcement is accelerating via continuous transaction controls, exemplified by Singapore's Peppol-based InvoiceNow mandates extending through 2031. On the trade policy front, high levels of trade friction persist as broad baseline tariffs, Section 122 expirations, and retaliatory levies force multinational enterprises to re-engineer global supply chain tax strategies.
