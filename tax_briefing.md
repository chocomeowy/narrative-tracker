# Tax Intelligence Briefing - 2026-09-24 04:01

The 2026 global tax landscape is defined by the rigid consolidation of the OECD Pillar Two 15% minimum tax, which is now moving from conceptual framework to active enforcement across major jurisdictions. Nations, particularly in the ASEAN region, are recalibrating their corporate tax incentives—balancing statutory rates like Singapore’s 17% with complex Domestic Top-up Tax regimes—to maintain FDI appeal while adhering to global standards. Simultaneously, digital tax administration is accelerating via mandatory e-invoicing, with Singapore’s InvoiceNow serving as a benchmark. Geopolitical trade volatility remains a persistent feature, as major economies continue to leverage tariffs as primary instruments of industrial and national economic strategy.
