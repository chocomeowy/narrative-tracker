# Tax Intelligence Briefing - 2026-09-21 22:01

The corporate and international tax policy landscape in mid-to-late 2026 is anchored by the maturation of OECD Pillar Two, backed by comprehensive datasets like the 2026 Corporate Tax Statistics that allow tax authorities to monitor effective tax rates and base erosion with high granularity. Regional tax authorities, particularly in dynamic trade hubs like Singapore and the broader ASEAN zone, continue to adapt their investment promotion frameworks by shifting from traditional statutory concessions to non-taxable rebates and tailored incentives that respect the 15% minimum floor. Simultaneously, digital tax enforcement through Peppol-based e-invoicing and continuous transaction controls (CTCs) is rapidly transforming compliance from a periodic reporting burden to real-time data integration. In parallel, international trade and customs enforcement remain volatile as courts evaluate the legal boundaries of emergency tariff authorities, creating ongoing complexity for global supply chain cost forecasting.
