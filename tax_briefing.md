# Tax Intelligence Briefing - 2026-09-22 04:01

The global corporate and international tax landscape in mid-to-late 2026 is defined by the rapid operationalization of OECD Pillar Two and the digitization of tax administration. Over 60 jurisdictions have formally enacted or are actively implementing the 15% global minimum tax framework, supported by the 2026 OECD Corporate Tax Statistics report which equips tax authorities with unprecedented visibility into effective tax rates and transfer pricing strategies. To maintain competitive FDI postures within this floor, ASEAN trade hubs, led by Singapore, are pivoting toward highly structured, non-taxable corporate income tax rebates and strategic cash grants. Concurrently, digital compliance is transitioning to real-time integration via mandated Peppol-standardized networks such as Singapore's InvoiceNow. On the trade front, unilateral tariff actions and executive-level negotiations continue to create volatile environments for supply chain forecasting, keeping trade policy in a highly elevated state of friction.
