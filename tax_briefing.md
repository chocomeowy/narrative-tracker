# Tax Intelligence Briefing - 2026-11-12 16:01

Global corporate tax policy in late 2026 is defined by a dual-track transition: the active domestic enforcement of the OECD Pillar Two 15% global minimum tax and the intensification of unilateral trade protectionism through aggressive tariff updates. While statutory headline corporate tax rates have largely stabilized across major jurisdictions, ending the two-decade 'race to the bottom,' tax administrations are aggressively pivoting toward digital infrastructure. Mandatory real-time e-invoicing networks, exemplified by Singapore's InvoiceNow and the Peppol PINT standard, are becoming the primary tool for combating indirect tax leakage. Simultaneously, ASEAN nations are recalibrating their foreign direct investment (FDI) frameworks to provide competitive incentives that remain compliant with the Global Anti-Base Erosion (GloBE) rules.
