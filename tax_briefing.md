# Tax Intelligence Briefing - 2026-11-14 16:02

As of late 2026, global tax policy has transitioned from the design of theoretical frameworks to the friction of operational enforcement. The stabilization of statutory corporate income tax rates—holding steady at roughly 21% in major economies like the US—belies a more aggressive underlying shift: the rise of Effective Tax Rates (ETRs) driven by OECD Pillar Two. Multinationals are now navigating a 'Post-Incentive' era where traditional tax holidays in ASEAN and emerging markets are being replaced by R&D credits and non-tax grants to remain compliant with the 15% global floor. Simultaneously, trade policy is becoming increasingly entwined with fiscal policy, as seen in the frequent, rapid updates to Harmonized Tariff Schedules (HTS) and the use of unilateral tariffs as primary tools for geopolitical leverage. Tax administration has also entered a real-time phase, with Singapore and other trade hubs mandating system-to-system e-invoicing (Peppol) to eliminate VAT/GST leakage.
