# Tax Intelligence Briefing - 2026-08-01 16:01

Global corporate tax and trade policy in 2026 is defined by a dual pressure of structural international tax reform and mounting trade protectionism. The operationalization of the OECD Pillar Two 15% global minimum tax is now live across key international jurisdictions, compelling multinational enterprises to re-evaluate effective tax rates and corporate structures. Concurrently, executive tariff mandates and unilateral trade duty escalations introduce heightened market uncertainty and global supply chain realignment. In response, national jurisdictions—particularly within ASEAN—are strategically leveraging targeted corporate tax incentives and effective rate relief to preserve competitiveness, while revenue authorities globally accelerate continuous transaction controls through mandatory Peppol e-invoicing networks such as Singapore's InvoiceNow.
