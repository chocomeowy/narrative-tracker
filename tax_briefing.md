# Tax Intelligence Briefing - 2026-07-26 10:01

In mid-2026, global tax policy is defined by a dual pressure of operational corporate tax reform and volatile international trade measures. Over 60 jurisdictions are actively enforcing OECD Pillar Two 15% global minimum tax rules, compelling multinational enterprises to adjust to domestic top-up taxes and GloBE reporting despite the ongoing non-participation of the United States. Concurrently, international trade taxation remains highly reactive as judicial rulings regarding IEEPA emergency tariffs trigger new Section 232 duties on critical commodities like metals and pharmaceuticals. To safeguard domestic revenues amidst these broader global realignments, major economic hubs across ASEAN and Europe are re-engineering regional tax incentives and establishing mandatory, real-time e-invoicing compliance frameworks such as Peppol-based InvoiceNow.
