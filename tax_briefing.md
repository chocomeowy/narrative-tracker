# Tax Intelligence Briefing - 2026-11-17 22:01

As of late 2026, the global corporate tax landscape has entered a period of structural consolidation. The OECD Pillar Two framework has transitioned from a theoretical policy goal to an operational reality for most multinational enterprises, effectively ending the decades-long race to the bottom in statutory corporate tax rates. In response to this new 15% effective tax floor, jurisdictions—particularly in the ASEAN region—are actively pivoting their competitive strategies from headline rate cuts to complex, Pillar Two-compliant investment incentives. Concurrently, tax administration is undergoing a mandatory digital transformation, characterized by the global adoption of Peppol-based real-time e-invoicing. While tax systems stabilize, global trade policy remains highly volatile, with protectionist tariff cycles emerging as a primary instrument for industrial policy and supply chain sovereignty.
