# Tax Intelligence Briefing - 2026-10-24 04:01

The global corporate tax policy landscape in late 2026 is defined by the full-scale operational implementation of OECD Pillar Two global minimum tax rules alongside continuous digital tax administration shifts and rising trade tariffs. As over 60 jurisdictions enforce the 15% GloBE floor, tax competition has shifted away from statutory rate reductions toward non-rate structural incentives and substance-based credits. Simultaneously, tax authorities are accelerating continuous transaction controls and digital compliance frameworks like mandatory e-invoicing and real-time GST reporting to improve revenue collection efficiency. Compounded by volatile trade protectionism and unilateral tariff shifts, multinational enterprises must harmonize cross-border tax compliance, supply chain logistics, and digital reporting systems.
