# Tax Intelligence Briefing - 2026-07-28 16:01

As of mid-2026, international tax policy and global supply chain compliance are defined by two main structural imperatives: widespread statutory enforcement of OECD Pillar Two's 15% minimum tax and aggressive trade tariff legal dynamics. With over 140 jurisdictions enforcing Pillar Two, traditional low-tax strategies are increasingly constrained, prompting regional hubs across ASEAN to offer targeted CIT rebates, grants, and strategic economic incentives to maintain FDI attraction. Simultaneously, unilateral trade measures, such as Section 232 tariff expansions and Court of International Trade rulings on tariff reliquidations, are reshaping international logistics risk. To enhance tax visibility and prevent leakage, revenue authorities are expediting mandatory digital tax compliance via Peppol-standard e-invoicing systems.
