# Tax Intelligence Briefing - 2026-09-20 16:01

The global corporate tax and trade policy landscape in 2026 is defined by operationalization, digital integration, and intensified legal friction. The OECD Pillar Two 15% global minimum tax is fully operational across approximately 140 jurisdictions, forcing multinational enterprises to manage complex GloBE information filings and domestic top-up rules. In response, regional hubs—particularly across ASEAN—are aggressively calibrating statutory corporate tax structures and deploying target grants, rebates, and incentives to preserve foreign direct investment attractiveness. Concurrently, tax administration is rapidly transitioning toward real-time enforcement via Continuous Transaction Controls and mandatory e-invoicing mandates, such as Singapore's GST InvoiceNow rollout taking effect in April 2026. Overseas trade policy remains highly volatile, as unilateral tariffs and reciprocal trade barriers trigger escalating legal challenges in national courts and force global supply chains to structurally adapt.
