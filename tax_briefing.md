# Tax Intelligence Briefing - 2026-11-26 16:01

The global tax landscape in late 2026 remains dominated by the intersection of geopolitical protectionism and aggressive administrative digitalization. While the OECD's Pillar Two framework faces mounting political resistance and uncertainty, particularly from the U.S., tax authorities worldwide are doubling down on structural compliance through mandated, real-time data transmission (e-invoicing). Simultaneously, tariffs have been elevated to a primary instrument of geopolitical statecraft, significantly increasing supply chain cost volatility and forcing multinational firms to integrate trade policy monitoring directly into their fiscal risk management strategies. The ASEAN region continues to balance these pressures by maintaining competitive, relatively stable corporate tax frameworks.
