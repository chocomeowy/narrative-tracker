# Tax Intelligence Briefing - 2026-08-18 10:01

In mid-2026, global corporate tax policy and international trade frameworks are undergoing structural transformations. The OECD Pillar Two 15% global minimum tax is fully operational across 140 jurisdictions, forcing multinational enterprises to navigate new domestic top-up taxes and effective tax rate benchmarks. ASEAN economies are responding by maintaining competitive headline statutory rates—such as Singapore's 17% rate—while utilizing targeted rebates and recalibrated incentive structures. Simultaneously, trade policy faces judicial and operational volatility following Court of International Trade rulings on executive emergency tariff powers (IEEPA). On the indirect tax front, continuous transaction controls and Peppol-based e-invoicing regimes, like Singapore's phased GST InvoiceNow mandate through 2031, are establishing automated real-time compliance as standard practice.
