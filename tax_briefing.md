# Tax Intelligence Briefing - 2026-07-30 16:01

Global corporate and international tax policy in mid-2026 is defined by the full-scale operationalization of the OECD Pillar Two 15% global minimum tax across approximately 140 jurisdictions, compelling multinational enterprises to navigate complex top-up tax liabilities. To protect economic competitiveness under this global floor, regional financial hubs such as Singapore and ASEAN partners are deploying strategic corporate income tax (CIT) rebates, cash grants, and targeted incentives. Concurrently, international trade remains volatile due to expanded Section 232 tariffs on metals, new pharmaceutical trade measures, and unilateral duty escalations. To tighten enforcement and revenue collection, tax authorities are simultaneously accelerating digital tax compliance through mandated PEPPOL-compliant real-time e-invoicing networks worldwide.
