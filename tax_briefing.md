# Tax Intelligence Briefing - 2026-09-16 16:01

As of 2026, the global corporate tax and trade environment continues to experience profound structural transformations. The OECD Pillar Two 15% global minimum tax is heavily operationalized, pushing large multinational enterprises past legislative compliance into complex GloBE filings, ETR tracking, and domestic top-up tax audits across more than 140 jurisdictions. Concurrently, regional tax authorities are fine-tuning fiscal incentives—such as Singapore's 17% corporate rate paired with structured CIT rebates and cash grants—to sustain FDI appeal under minimum tax constraints. In parallel, continuous transaction controls and mandatory electronic invoicing frameworks, like Singapore's Peppol-based GST InvoiceNow mandates, are reshaping tax administration. Finally, international trade continues to face intense friction marked by Section 301 tariffs, aggressive retaliatory counter-tariffs up to 50% on industrial goods, and ongoing free trade expansion.
