# Tax Intelligence Briefing - 2026-08-02 16:01

Global corporate tax and trade policy in 2026 is defined by the maturing operationalization of the OECD Pillar Two 15 percent global minimum tax across more than 60 enacted jurisdictions, alongside domestic statutory adjustments like the US One Big Beautiful Bill Act and targeted European rate modifications. In tandem, sovereign entities maintain investment competitiveness through territorial tax frameworks and targeted corporate tax rebates, notably Singapore's CIT cash grants and ASEAN incentive structures. High trade volatility continues as unilateral tariffs and retaliatory duties drive corporate duty recovery strategies and judicial challenges, reflected in significant corporate tariff refunds. Concurrently, tax administrations are formalizing mandatory digital compliance through Peppol-based Continuous Transaction Controls (CTC) and phased GST InvoiceNow mandates.
