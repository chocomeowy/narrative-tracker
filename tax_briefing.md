# Tax Intelligence Briefing - 2026-07-26 16:01

In mid-2026, global tax policy is defined by dual pressures of operational corporate tax reform and persistent tariff friction. Over 60 jurisdictions actively enforce OECD Pillar Two 15% global minimum tax rules, driving MNEs to adapt to domestic top-up taxes and GloBE reporting while navigating U.S. non-participation. Concurrently, international trade taxation faces heightened friction as expanded Section 232 duties targeting metals and pharmaceutical imports alongside fresh bilateral tariffs provoke international pushback. To protect domestic revenue bases amidst global realignments, major economic hubs across ASEAN and Europe are re-engineering corporate tax incentives and enforcing mandatory, real-time e-invoicing compliance frameworks such as Peppol-based InvoiceNow.
