# Tax Intelligence Briefing - 2026-07-28 10:01

As of July 2026, global corporate tax policy and international trade compliance are dominated by dual pressures: aggressive unilateral tariff expansions and strict statutory enforcement of multilateral tax frameworks. The United States has expanded unilateral tariffs with 10% to 12.5% import duties targeting 60 trading partners, forcing multinational entities to overhaul supply chain tax structures. Simultaneously, the OECD Pillar Two 15% global minimum tax regime continues its widespread statutory adoption across over 140 jurisdictions, constraining traditional low-tax haven strategies. In response, regional hubs across ASEAN and Singapore are reforming corporate income tax structures using non-taxable CIT rebates, grants, and strategic incentives to preserve FDI attractiveness. Furthermore, revenue authorities are accelerating mandatory digital compliance through standardized Peppol-based e-invoicing frameworks to ensure real-time transactional transparency.
