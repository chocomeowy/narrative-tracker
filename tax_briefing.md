## Intelligence Map Update - May 4, 2026

**Executive Briefing:**

The global tax landscape remains highly dynamic, driven by the ongoing implementation of Pillar Two and escalating trade tensions, particularly those initiated by the US. The US’s divergence from OECD multilateral agreements, adopting a side-by-side approach to Pillar Two and aggressively pursuing new tariffs, introduces significant uncertainty for multinational enterprises. The global minimum tax regime is solidifying, with over 140 jurisdictions enforcing the rules, while the US is implementing its own parallel system. Simultaneously, a resurgence of trade tariffs, including potential investigations into numerous countries’ trade practices, is disrupting global commerce. Singapore continues to be a competitive tax jurisdiction, and its leadership in digital tax transformation through the expansion of its e-invoicing initiative (Peppol PINT SG) is noteworthy. Businesses must proactively adapt to these shifts, focusing on global minimum tax calculations, tariff impact assessments, and digital tax compliance. The interplay between these factors demands a flexible and informed international tax strategy. The ASEAN region presents a varied tax landscape, with Singapore maintaining a competitive edge.
