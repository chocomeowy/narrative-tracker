# Tax Intelligence Briefing - 2026-11-15 16:01

As of late 2026, global tax policy is defined by structural stabilization in corporate income tax rates paired with heightened operational and trade compliance friction. Broad international consensus on the OECD Pillar Two 15% global minimum tax has successfully halted the multi-decade race-to-the-bottom in statutory corporate tax rates. In response, regional economies—notably across ASEAN—are replacing legacy tax holidays with targeted, performance-based reinvestment allowances. Concurrently, tax authorities worldwide are enforcing mandatory e-invoicing networks (such as Singapore's InvoiceNow mandate) to automate real-time audit control, while rising global protectionist tariffs introduce new supply chain tax costs and cross-border trade friction.
