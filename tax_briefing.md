# Tax Intelligence Briefing - 2026-11-14 22:01

As of late 2026, global tax policy continues its rapid evolution toward operational enforcement, real-time digital compliance, and structural defense of tax bases. Statutory corporate tax rates, anchored by a baseline of 21% in major economies like the US, remain stable, but the implementation of OECD Pillar Two's 15% global minimum tax is forcing multinational enterprises to re-engineer their effective tax rates and compliance infrastructure. Concurrently, regional blocs like ASEAN are actively overhauling their tax incentive regimes—moving away from traditional tax holidays toward reinvestment credits and grants—to maintain foreign direct investment attractiveness without triggering top-up taxes. Furthermore, trade and fiscal policies are closely intertwined through escalating global tariffs and volatile Harmonized Tariff Schedules, while revenue authorities in jurisdictions like Singapore expand real-time e-invoicing mandates (InvoiceNow/Peppol) to eliminate indirect tax leakage.
