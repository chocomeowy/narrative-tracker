# Tax Intelligence Briefing - 2026-08-07 22:01

Global tax policy in 2026 is defined by the full operationalization of the OECD Pillar Two 15 percent global minimum tax across nearly 140 jurisdictions, forcing multinational enterprises to navigate complex top-up tax mechanics. Concurrently, major judicial interventions--such as Supreme Court rulings invalidating executive tariff measures and triggering roughly $100 billion in government refunds--highlight intense legal and supply chain volatility in international trade. Regionally, tax authorities in hubs like ASEAN are recalibrating statutory corporate tax structures and enterprise rebates to balance competitiveness against global reform pressures. Meanwhile, indirect tax administration is undergoing a rapid digital evolution through mandatory e-invoicing frameworks and continuous transaction controls, led by Peppol-based systems such as Singapore's InvoiceNow.
