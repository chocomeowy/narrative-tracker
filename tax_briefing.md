# Tax Intelligence Briefing - 2026-10-29 10:01

In late 2026, global corporate tax policy is anchored by the full operational implementation of OECD Pillar Two rules across over 60 jurisdictions, putting a firm floor under statutory corporate income tax rates worldwide and bringing an end to the multi-decade tax rate race to the bottom. In response, regional hubs—notably across ASEAN—are actively restructuring their foreign investment incentives away from traditional tax holidays toward substance-based models, research credits, and qualified domestic top-up taxes. Simultaneously, tax administrations are rapidly deploying mandatory real-time e-invoicing and Continuous Transaction Controls (CTCs)—such as Singapore's GST InvoiceNow network—to close indirect tax gaps. In parallel, dynamic trade policy adjustments and sectoral tariffs driven by national security considerations continue to force MNEs to restructure cross-border trade and supply chain architectures.
