# Tax Intelligence Briefing - 2026-10-05 04:01

The corporate and international tax policy landscape in late 2026 is defined by two major structural forces: the statutory enforcement of the OECD Pillar Two 15% global minimum tax, backed by empirical compliance tracking in the 2026 Corporate Tax Statistics, and a sharp escalation in global trade tariffs. These regulatory shifts are accompanied by digital tax enforcement, notably the mandatory transition to real-time e-invoicing networks like Singapore's InvoiceNow. Concurrently, regional blocs like ASEAN are recalibrating their corporate tax rates, withholding taxes, and investment incentives to preserve competitiveness in a highly protective global trade environment.
