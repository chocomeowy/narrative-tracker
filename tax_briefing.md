# Tax Intelligence Briefing - 2026-09-12 10:01

The corporate and international tax policy landscape in late 2026 is defined by the operationalization of multilateral accords alongside regional statutory re-alignments. As approximately 140 countries actively enforce or transition to the OECD Pillar Two 15% global minimum tax (GloBE) framework, multinational enterprises (MNEs) are shifting focus from strategic planning to direct compliance. The global average composite effective tax rate (EATR) has stabilized at 20.5%, supported by targeted local incentives and structural adjustments such as Portugal's progressive rate reduction starting in 2026 and Singapore's transitional rebates. Simultaneously, domestic tax systems are digitizing rapidly: continuous transaction control mandates, exemplifying by Singapore's Peppol-based InvoiceNow system-to-system exchange, are becoming standard practice for tax administrations globally. While US tax structures remain largely stable under the recent 'One Big Beautiful Bill Act,' global trade operations continue to grapple with shifting tariffs and bilateral negotiations, compelling enterprises to prioritize real-time supply chain compliance.
